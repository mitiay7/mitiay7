# Все кандидаты первой волны (рабочие материалы, англ.)

Сырые результаты: описание кандидата и оценки трёх скептиков (1–10). Разведка рынка в первой волне шла без поиска, поэтому сведения о конкурентах здесь не проверены.


## Софт: 20 кандидатов


### c01 Kin Voice (Голос семьи) - daily question, sign of life and family voice memoir — avg 5

- Direction: Family safety net
- Idea: One warm daily question or family postcard for a parent living alone; her reply is today's sign of life and a growing voice memoir.
- How: Each morning the elder gets one warm question (often from grandchildren) or a family photo postcard, via Telegram/MAX/VK bot or a one-screen app. A voice-note answer, one tap, or on Android a passive first unlock turns the family's tick green. Silence past her learned hour triggers a re-ping, then an escalation ladder to relatives by push, messenger and email. Answers become transcripts, weekly chapters and a searchable 'ask grandma' archive of her real clips; relatives' short notes return as a Sunday radio episode. No calls, no rescue promises.
- Why pay instead of DIY: A dead-man timer must run 24/7 outside the phone it watches, on a non-technical parent's device, with store-signed apps, push credentials and multi-channel escalation. The voice archive is irreplaceable shared state across three generations; a home-made cron fails silently exactly when it matters, and nobody wants to host a decade of grandma's voice.
- Price: Free: daily question, OK tick, alert to one relative, 30 days of audio. Family circle (8 relatives, 2 elders): $12/year or 890-990 RUB/year via SBP, $9.99 by store billing; giftable 'for mum'; elder's side always free. Annual only. Printed book and SMS/voice escalation as prepaid cost-plus credits.
- Wow: Mum's daily 'I'm fine' stops being a chore: it is her voice telling a story you never heard, saved forever. If she stays silent, the whole family knows by noon; in twenty years grandchildren can still ask her.
- Jury/moat 5/10: The real rival is not DIY code but the free family chat, where mum's morning voice note already lives forever. On the messenger path there is no gatekept asset: a bot plus speech-to-text is a weekend clone. Lock-in is per family, never cross-user, and a solo founder is an implausible 20-year custodian of grandma's voice. FIX: Sell custody and gatekept reach, not the bot. Mirror every clip to the family's own cloud drive so the archive outlives the service; make paid escalation run through registered SMS/voice senders and landline calls; let one circle span both spouses' parents so circles overlap across families; tune the question bank on pooled response rates.
- Jury/economics 5/10: Revenue scales with paying circles, but alerts, support and liability scale with every person. With the safety layer free and one $12 charge covering ten people, 100k users yield roughly 750-1,250 paying circles: $7-12k a year. Meanwhile one messenger block turns every tick red at once, and the founder holds pensioners' voices and family graphs. FIX: Sell the memoir, not the alarm: a gifted family voice archive where the tick reads 'last heard from' and escalation is an opt-in nudge with outage suppression. Launch in one jurisdiction on one bot channel, SBP annual prepay without auto-renewal, no native app. Host and encrypt audio locally, offer export. Price $18-20 per circle.
- Jury/demand 5/10: The daily ritual depends on the hardest user to retain: a 70-year-old who must voice-answer a bot 365 times a year. The sign of life is already free: her 'good morning' postcard in the family chat, 'last seen' status. Once she drops to one tap, the archive stops growing, and with it the only reason to pay or renew. FIX: Sell the memoir, not the alarm: a giftable 'Mum's voice' archive with weekly grandchild-authored questions. Put the bot inside the existing family chat, so her usual 'good morning' counts as the sign of life. End every shared story card with 'ask your own grandma this' to seed new circles. Charge at day 30 to keep the archive.

### c04 Seatbelt - outside guardian for vibe-coded apps (proven backups, exposure watch, fleet warnings) — avg 4.3

- Direction: Builder safety net
- Idea: One prompt gives a live vibe-coded app what must live outside it: agent-proof backups with restore proof, daily exposure checks and fleet-wide early warnings.
- How: Paste 'add Seatbelt': the agent installs an MCP server/skill, proves domain ownership and creates a read-only database role. From outside, it takes encrypted nightly dumps into immutable storage (deletion needs owner confirmation plus a 7-day delay) and restores one into a scratch Postgres to compare row counts. Daily probes check readable tables, secrets in bundles, exposed .env/.git, sign-up email arrival, TLS, domain and pause countdowns. A hole or breakage found in one member's app triggers rechecks for everyone on that stack, with paste-ready fix prompts. Monthly signed evidence report.
- Why pay instead of DIY: A backup script written by the same agent, in the same project with the same credentials, dies in the same incident; an agent cannot attest its own work. Independent custody, third-party restore drills and signed evidence cannot be prompted into existence. Fleet telemetry across thousands of apps tells 'your bug' from 'platform outage' and yields herd-immunity rules.
- Price: Free: instant public-URL scan, one app with weekly checks and one on-demand backup kept seven days; export always free. $19/year per account for three apps (990-1,490 RUB via SBP); extra app $5/year. Prepaid $5 wallet: guided restore $2-3, deep scan $1-2, extra storage. Agencies $5-9/month for 25 apps. No monthly card plan.
- Wow: 'Last night we really restored your database: 14 tables, 8,412 rows, intact - and your agent physically cannot delete this copy.' Plus: 'not your code: 41 apps on your stack broke at 14:05; this prompt fixed 37.'
- Jury/moat 5/10: Only one real lock exists - backups must sit outside the project - and it is not gatekept: an agent can wire GitHub Actions to an object-locked bucket with a write-only key in 20 minutes, BackupDrill already sells restore drills, and Supabase, Lovable or Neon can bundle free-tier backups overnight. Those who grasp custody can DIY; those who cannot don't pay. FIX: Narrow to a platform-neutral 'restore witness' vault: customer-held encryption keys (Seatbelt stores ciphertext; drills run in ephemeral workers), delayed deletion, signed restore evidence. Lead with agencies and freelancers who must show clients proof - evidence history compounds and platforms cannot attest themselves. Make scans a free funnel only; drop fleet claims until 5,000 installs.
- Jury/economics 4/10: Restore proofs and nightly pulls require Seatbelt to hold standing database credentials and decryptable dumps for thousands of third-party apps, free users included. A solo founder netting about $17 per payer-year becomes a GDPR processor and 152-FZ operator with uncapped breach liability; per-tenant encryption does not help when the service itself must decrypt. FIX: Invert custody: a customer-side job pushes dumps encrypted to the owner's public key into an append-only, delay-delete vault via a write-only token; Seatbelt stores no credentials and cannot read data. Verify by row-count manifest and missed-heartbeat alerts. Supabase direct only, ownership-verified scans, fully self-serve restore, storage quota by GB, no ruble edition until hosted in Russia.
- Jury/demand 4/10: The paying window is transient and tiny: an app must be live, hold data worth protecting, yet earn too little to justify Supabase Pro at $25/month. Dead projects churn out, successful ones graduate to bundled backups. That leaves thousands of payers, not millions - and prevention sold to non-technical builders who have not yet been burned rarely converts. FIX: Make freelancers and agencies the payer ($5-9/month per client portfolio) and the client-facing monthly Restore Proof the loop: clients see it, other clients demand it. Acquire at felt moments - Supabase pause warnings, broken sign-up emails - with pause and breakage alerts free, custody paid. Treat it as the B2B tail of a broader product, not the mass flagship.

### c05 Sealed Envelope (Конверт / Pulse) - zero-knowledge if-I-go-silent release — avg 4.3

- Direction: Family safety net
- Idea: An encrypted 'if something happens to me' envelope that reaches your chosen people only if you go silent - unreadable even to the service.
- How: A ten-minute guided checklist builds your map: accounts and where keys live, policies, autopays, contacts, pet and home instructions, letters. It is encrypted on the device; trustees hold key shares in advance, the server holds only ciphertext. A light heartbeat (any app open, a weekly or monthly one-tap ping; optionally a daily 'I'm OK' tick the circle sees) keeps it sealed. Silence starts a ladder you set: reminders, trustee confirmation, then release after 7-60 days. Short timers cover hikes, surgery and solo trips. Quarterly 'still accurate?' nudges.
- Why pay instead of DIY: A self-built dead-man's switch dies with its builder: the server bill lapses, the domain expires, nobody fixes the cron. Release must come from a neutral, prepaid third party that outlives the user's attention, holds ciphertext but no key, has deliverability to reach trustees, and publishes uptime. Independence and continuity are the product.
- Price: Free: one trustee, text-only map, one short timer. $12/year personal, $19/year couple or household (two owners, 6 recipients, 1 GB of scans); five-year prepay $49; 990 RUB/year via SBP. Every payment includes a 12-month grace runway, so a dead card never deletes an envelope. Annual only; recipients free.
- Wow: The one thing a builder cannot build for himself: software that keeps working after he no longer can. Ten minutes and a dollar a month close the scariest gap in family life - and the provider mathematically cannot read a word.
- Jury/moat 4/10: The moat is "a neutral party that outlives you", and a solo founder is the least durable party in the category. Google's Inactive Account Manager and Bitwarden/Proton emergency access already offer free or bundled, institution-backed release where the secrets already live; one "release on inactivity" toggle from a password manager erases the remaining gap. FIX: Stop selling a standalone vault. Make the envelope a bundled feature of the daily check-in product (c01), holding a "where everything is" map that points to password-manager emergency access rather than replacing it. Manufacture continuity structurally: escrowed five-year runway, a second independent release operator, and offline trustee decryption that works if the service dies.
- Jury/economics 5/10: Silence is mostly churn, not death. At roughly 36% twelve-month retention, tens of thousands of abandoned accounts per 100k enter the ladder yearly, pinging trustees or releasing to stale ones (ex-partners). Zero-knowledge means support cannot repair a lost key share, and a failed release surfaces only after death: unbounded liability against $12. FIX: Remove the catastrophic tail: store a "where things are" map, never secrets, and release only on trustee request with an owner veto window, not silence alone. Paid tier funds SMS/voice escalation and yearly trustee re-verification; inactive free envelopes expire unreleased. Launch globally via a merchant of record; defer Russia. Publish audit and wind-down escrow first.
- Jury/demand 4/10: The buyer never experiences the value: the payoff arrives only after death or incapacity, so there is no daily use, no felt wow and nothing to tell friends. People procrastinate death admin even when it is free (Google, Apple, password-manager emergency access), and the category's direct-to-consumer attempts died or retreated to advisor channels. FIX: Do not launch standalone. Make the envelope the paid tier of the daily 'I'm OK' family check-in (c01): the check-in supplies habit, visibility and invites; the envelope converts at life triggers (newborn, mortgage, surgery, trip). Sell per household at $12-19/year, show a quarterly 'sealed and verified' proof, and add notaries or insurers as a later channel.

### c06 Last Call - household deadline and money-leak sentinel — avg 4.3

- Direction: Household money and deadlines
- Idea: Forward any bill, policy, contract or 'we'll get back to you'; it warns the right family member at the last useful moment, with the action ready.
- How: Each household gets a private forwarding address, a chat bot and an MCP connector for family members' AI assistants. Send a photo, PDF, email or voice note; OCR plus a small model extracts dates, amounts, notice periods and the owner. A deterministic scheduler watches trial ends, auto-renewals, insurance and ID expiry, return and fine-discount windows, and promises companies made to you. Alerts arrive only when action pays, with a ready cancellation letter, haggle script or follow-up, escalating to the other adult. No bank login, no inbox access.
- Why pay instead of DIY: Maintained shared data, not code: per-merchant cancellation paths, notice rules, statutory clocks and real company response times refreshed by all users' outcomes. Plus shared state across several relatives and rival AI vendors, inbound-email deliverability, 24/7 scheduling and an accumulating family archive - a self-builder becomes the family's on-call operator.
- Price: Free: 10 live watched items plus a one-off statement audit. Household plan $12/year ('$1 a month, billed yearly'; $6 regional; 790 RUB via SBP): six members, 300 items, 60-100 pages a month. Guarantee: if flagged avoidable charges total less than the fee, the next year is free. Never a cut of savings.
- Wow: Photograph the paper pile once and never think about dates again: 'cancel the gym by Friday, letter attached, saves $420'. A running counter - 'saved you $214, cost you $12' - and a promise that if it finds less than its fee, you do not pay again.
- Jury/moat 4/10: The paid core - extract a date, schedule an alert, draft a letter - is already a weekend clone (217 subscription-tracker repos, Gemini-Vision expiry bots) and a free platform habit: photograph the bill into Gemini/ChatGPT, or let Gmail's AI Inbox surface it. The claimed per-merchant outcome dataset does not exist at launch, and forwarding users rarely report outcomes. FIX: Pick one jurisdiction (Germany/UK or CIS) and make the product the gatekept act, not the reminder: cancellation, claim and appeal letters dispatched through your pooled registered-mail/e-delivery contracts with timestamped proof of delivery, a small per-send fee plus $12/year. Dispatch flowing through you generates response-time data automatically. Expose alerts via calendar feed and MCP so platforms become channels.
- Jury/economics 5/10: The infrastructure margin is real, but liability is not priced: for $12 a year it accepts responsibility for deadlines worth $30-600 each, extracted by OCR from messy documents, while holding IDs, policies and statements for six people per household. One misread date or one breach costs more than years of that household's revenue - and one founder carries it. FIX: Launch in one jurisdiction with one intake path (email forwarding plus web upload) and a narrow item set: trials, auto-renewals, insurance and ID expiry. Users confirm every extracted date; store fields only, delete originals. Sell reminders, not legal correctness - generic letter templates, 'flagged' not 'saved'. Drop the multi-country merchant database and guarantee until volume exists.
- Jury/demand 4/10: It's a sentinel that only works if the user keeps feeding it. Deadlines arrive a dozen times a year, not daily; inbox-borne ones are being absorbed free by Gmail, banks and Госуслуги; the rest need a photographing chore few perform. Unfed accounts stay silent, get forgotten, and the household invite loop never crosses households. FIX: Make intake ten-second and messenger-native: a Telegram/share-sheet bot where one screenshot of a trial, fine or policy becomes a watched deadline. Launch in one market (CIS, or Germany/UK notice periods). Bundle with scam-check and a family OK check-in for weekly touches. Cut the free tier to five items; replace the renew-free guarantee with refund on request.

### c07 Handshake (По рукам) - neutral witness, polite reminders and a verified job record — avg 4.3

- Direction: Neutral witness and treasurer
- Idea: Two taps turn any informal deal - a loan, a deposit, a freelance job - into a witnessed record; a neutral bot does the reminding and confirmed jobs build a portable reputation.
- How: Type or dictate the deal to the bot ('lent Anton 15,000 until 1 March'); a small model makes a one-screen card. The counterparty opens a link, needs no install and taps 'Agreed'. Both receive an identical record, hashed and publicly timestamped. The neutral bot then sends capped, polite reminders, logs partial repayments confirmed by both, and closes the deal. Photo handovers work the same way. For freelancers, client-confirmed 'Done as agreed' jobs accumulate on a public verified profile. Payments stay person-to-person; evidence pack exports as PDF.
- Why pay instead of DIY: Independence is the product: a witness you host yourself proves nothing to the other side, and a reminder from 'your own bot' is still you nagging. The counterparty confirms only on neutral ground; the verified job record exists only inside a trusted common registry. A self-hosted witness is a contradiction.
- Price: Free: three open handshakes; counterparties and clients always free. $9-12/year (490-690 RUB via SBP, or Stars): unlimited handshakes, reminder schedules, photo handovers, full verified profile, exports. Pay-per-use: sealed evidence pack 49 RUB / $0.49 from a $3-5 prepaid wallet. No percentage of any amount, ever.
- Wow: You never again write 'hey, about that money...': someone neutral does it, and both hold the same witnessed record. For freelancers: 'strangers see 143 verified jobs under my name, and no marketplace took 20%'.
- Jury/moat 5/10: Neutrality is not an asset an unknown solo founder owns: any weekend clone is equally "neutral", while banks (which see the actual repayment) and Госключ are far more trusted. The one compounding asset, the verified-job registry, faces a cold start and cheap collusive confirmations; personal lenders rarely exceed three free handshakes, so nobody needs to pay. FIX: Make tradespeople the paying core and lending a free viral wedge. Anchor every verified job to a hard-to-fake external artefact (ФНС "Мой налог" чек, SBP receipt) so fakes cost real tax. Dominate one vertical and city first, with embeddable badges for Avito ads, on your own web domain rather than Telegram, with openly auditable proofs.
- Jury/economics 4/10: The paid core - a third party messaging a debtor about someone else's money - is regulated debt-collection territory (Russia's 230-FZ reserves overdue-debt contact for banks and registered collectors; FDCPA-type rules elsewhere), while storing non-users' debt data makes the founder a 152-FZ/GDPR operator. One fine or one breach outweighs thousands of $10 payer-years. FIX: Stop contacting debtors for lenders: send only pre-due self-reminders each party opted into; after the due date, hand the lender a draft to forward. Make the paid product the tradesperson job record anchored to 'Мой налог' receipts, web-first, no SMS, storing hashes rather than deal text, with a legal opinion on 230-FZ/152-FZ before launch.
- Jury/demand 4/10: Not daily, not continuous: an average person makes one or two witness-worthy informal deals a year, and three free open handshakes cover them all. Asking a friend to 'confirm' a loan signals the very distrust the product promises to remove, so the loop stalls exactly where need is highest; debtors nagged by a bot do not evangelise. FIX: Flip the core: sell a 'job passport' to самозанятые and tradespeople (490-990 RUB/year) - client-confirmed completions with photos, anchored to 'Мой налог' чеки, shown via link/QR inside their Avito ads and chats. Make personal loans and deposits unlimited and free as the viral wedge; monetise them only via 49 RUB evidence packs. Web-link-first, not Telegram-only.

### c12 Heads-Up Radar - recalls, settlements and refunds that are actually yours — avg 4.3

- Direction: Household money and deadlines
- Idea: Tell it what you own and use; it watches every recall, settlement, breach notice and refund programme and pings only when one is yours.
- How: Two-minute setup: car VIN or model, brands of appliances, baby gear and phones, services you hold accounts with, postcode - typed or photographed. Once a day the service ingests official open feeds (NHTSA, CPSC, FDA, EU Safety Gate, UK OPSS), court-approved settlement notices and public breach registers, normalises each notice once with a small model and matches it against all inventories. A hit produces one message: what, why it concerns you, the deadline and the official fix or claim link. Quiet months get a 'nothing found' receipt.
- Why pay instead of DIY: Fresh data, computed once for everyone: dozens of feeds and notice formats that change constantly, entity matching (model variants, brand owners, date ranges) curated continuously, and crowd confirmations ('I got $38'). A personal scraper needs the same upkeep for one household; here marginal cost per subscriber is near zero, so $1 always beats building.
- Price: Free: one car plus 5 items; safety recalls are always free. $9.99/year household plan (in-app purchase or merchant of record; $5 regional) unlocks unlimited inventory, money alerts (settlements, refunds, extended warranties) and family members. Launch offer: capped $19 founder lifetime pass. No commission on claims, ever.
- Wow: It pays for itself the first time it finds you $30 - or tells you the car seat your child sits in was recalled yesterday. 'Why does nobody already tell me this for a dollar?'
- Jury/moat 4/10: Every input is a free public feed and every leg already has a free incumbent, while the scarce asset - knowing what a household actually bought - sits with Amazon, Gmail, carmakers and retailers, not you. Safety must stay free, so the paid tier is exactly what Settlemate, ad-funded newsletters and a scheduled ChatGPT/Gemini task give away. FIX: Own the two assets nobody publishes: a scam-screened, deadline-tracked settlement and refund registry (the US has no official one) and a product-identity graph fed by forwarded receipts, so inventory builds itself. Sell it as one module of a broader household-deadlines watcher (warranties, returns, renewals), license the matching API to parenting apps, insurers and retailers, and lead with UK/EU.
- Jury/economics 5/10: The only paid leg - settlement and refund alerts - is the one with no official feed: it needs continuous human curation and scam-screening by one person, competes with free ad-funded rivals, and hits rarely. Safety must stay free, so 1-2% conversion at about $8.50 net yields roughly $8-17k a year per 100k users, plus silent-renewal disputes. FIX: Stop selling it standalone: make recall and settlement matching the free acquisition feature of a broader household-deadlines product with weekly value (warranties, renewals, document expiries) and charge $9.99/year for that. Launch US recalls only, require model numbers for precise matches, claim "official feeds we monitor" rather than "every", send renewal reminders, and drop the lifetime pass.
- Jury/demand 4/10: A relevant hit reaches a household perhaps once or twice a year, safety alerts must stay free, and every leg already has a free incumbent (SaferCar, CARFAX, Amazon, HIBP, Settlemate, Top Class Actions). The paid layer is rare settlement money that typically pays out after the renewal date, so few convert and fewer renew. FIX: Demote it to a free acquisition module inside a broader household sentinel (document deadlines, renewals, warranties) that gives monthly touches. Build the inventory automatically from forwarded receipts. Charge $10-19/year for the bundle, not for alerts. Launch in UK/EU, where no rival app appears to exist, leading with free embeddable checkers.

### c13 Fair Price Club (ParPrice) - crowd benchmark of what others really pay — avg 4.3

- Direction: Household money and deadlines
- Idea: A give-to-get club: forward a bill or renewal notice, see what people on the same plan really pay, and get the exact words that won them a discount.
- How: Forward a bill photo, PDF or tariff screenshot to a bot or address. OCR plus a small model extracts provider, plan, price, city, tenure and promo end date; identity is discarded. You instantly see your percentile among identical plans, today's public new-customer offer, and retention scripts ranked by reported success. Then it watches: alerts when a price-rise wave hits your provider ('1,240 users got +12% this week'), when the crowd median drops, and thirty days before each renewal. One tap after your call reports the outcome, sharpening everyone's benchmark.
- Why pay instead of DIY: Crowd data: a builder owns exactly one data point - their own bill. Real paid prices and retention offers are published nowhere and cannot be scraped; they exist only inside other households' documents. Costco logic keeps it neutral: revenue only from membership, never switching commissions. A private copy is permanently empty.
- Price: Give-to-get: contribute one bill, see that category's benchmark free. Membership $10-12/year per household (790-990 RUB via SBP; $6 regional), annual only: all contracts watched, wave warnings, renewal alerts and scripts, calendar feed. Flat fee, never a share of savings or switching commission. Automatic refund if no saving is found in year one.
- Wow: 'People on your exact plan pay 31% less. Here is the sentence that got them the discount.' Glassdoor for bills: the first time an ordinary customer sees the other side's price list.
- Jury/moat 5/10: The moat exists only after the dataset does, and monetisation misses it: the un-buildable crowd benchmark is given away for one bill and leaked through public SEO pages, while the paid layer (renewal reminders, scripts) is free in any calendar or LLM. Audience-holders (MSE, T-Bank, Rocket Money) can out-collect a solo founder with one email. FIX: Paywall freshness, not existence: publish 90-day-old aggregates free, sell the live retention-offer feed and price-wave alerts. Make contribution passive (auto-forward monthly e-bills to a personal address) so data refreshes without effort. Launch one dense market (Russian mobile/home internet, top cities), accept document-verified submissions only, and seed via a media or consumer-community partnership.
- Jury/economics 4/10: The refund promise self-destructs: a percentile benchmark tells roughly half of members they already pay at or below the median, so 'no saving found' refunds are structural, unverifiable and gameable, and payment fees on refunded charges are not returned. Meanwhile nothing is sellable until each plan-city cell holds about 30 bills, which takes years. FIX: Drop the refund guarantee and annual membership. Keep benchmark pages free; sell a 199-299 RUB one-off 'renewal pass' (percentile, scripts, 60-day watch) via SBP at the moment of need. One country, one category. Extract in-country, store fields only, delete images. Publish only cells with 30+ bills, labelled user-reported, with no named-operator accusations.
- Jury/demand 4/10: It is an episodic need (one to four renewal moments a year per household) wrapped in a crowd product that is empty on day one. A solo founder must gather about 30 bills per provider-plan-city cell before any 'wow' exists; Consumer Reports, with a national brand, got only ~22k bills. First-time users meet 'not enough data', and churn silently. FIX: Stop selling it as a standalone membership. Make the benchmark a free public page for one nationally priced category (home internet), seeded with list prices and typed three-field entries, bill optional. Monetise as a feature of a continuous household sentinel bundle, or as a small pay-after-saving unlock at renewal time.

### c15 Drawer Guard - an old phone becomes a home sentinel — avg 4.3

- Direction: Family safety net
- Idea: An old phone left plugged in at home or the dacha reports power cuts, cold, noise and alarms, even when the house goes offline.
- How: Install the app on a spare Android phone, leave it on a charger, scan a QR to pair with your messenger or email. The phone reports every few minutes: mains power (charging state), temperature trend, sound level, on-device recognition of smoke/CO alarm beeps, Wi-Fi and mobile signal. The server alerts on events and on silence - power and internet both gone. With a SIM the phone says 'on battery, 80%'. Family members share one home; a monthly 'all quiet' summary proves vigilance. Audio never leaves the phone.
- Why pay instead of DIY: Detecting silence needs a vantage point outside the house, alive 24/7 when home power and internet are dead. Reliable background sensing needs a store-published app with push credentials, and turning battery temperature into room temperature needs calibration curves pooled from thousands of phone models. One household's script has none of these.
- Price: Free: one phone, hourly heartbeat, email alerts. Home plan $9.99/year via Google Play (590 RUB via SBP): 3 sensor phones, 5-minute heartbeat, alarm-sound detection, 6 family watchers, all channels. Host plan $19/year per rental property with noise reports. SMS alerts as prepaid cost-plus credits.
- Wow: The forgotten phone in your drawer becomes the sensor others sell for $100 plus a monthly fee: it tells you the heating failed at the dacha or the smoke alarm is beeping - for $10 a year.
- Jury/moat 4/10: None of the three claimed locks holds. The sensor phone only makes outbound pings, so a DIYer needs no store listing or push credentials: MacroDroid or a sideloaded APK plus free Healthchecks.io and a Telegram bot reproduces power-plus-silence alerts in an hour. Battery temperature is a poor room proxy, and nothing compounds across households. FIX: Add a lock that grows with density: pool all sentinels into a live per-village/building outage map ('only your house' vs 'whole street; neighbours restored 14:02') with shareable community channels. Drop battery-temperature claims; pair a $5 BLE thermometer instead. Lead with power-plus-silence, pre-registered SMS/voice escalation, and a maintained per-model reliability matrix.
- Jury/economics 5/10: The sensor is ageing hardware nobody controls. Most phones stay off once the battery drains in a long outage, so a dacha sentinel dies until someone visits; silence cannot separate power, internet or a killed app. Every false alarm is a ticket against $8.49 net a year, and drawer phones lack the charge limit the fire mitigation needs. FIX: Ship power-plus-silence only: no microphone, no smoke, pet or temperature claims. On battery, drop to an hourly low-power beat and send a 'going dark, needs manual restart' warning. Label silence using neighbouring homes' heartbeats. Self-monitor battery heat and retire unsafe phones. Cut free to daily checks. Launch one stack first, Play globally or RuStore/SBP in Russia, not both.
- Jury/demand 4/10: The need is real but rare and stacked behind filters: an unattended home that matters, a working spare Android, internet on site, and willingness to leave an ageing lithium battery charging in an empty house. Survivors get most of the value from the free tier, a free SvitloBot, or a $10-40 smart plug - so few pay and nothing spreads. FIX: Do not sell it standalone. Ship the old-phone sentinel as a free acquisition hook and module of the household guardian plan, leading with power-plus-silence in outage-prone markets via Telegram. Drop temperature claims; support a cheap paired Bluetooth thermometer instead. Charge for voice-call/SMS escalation, multiple homes and elderly-parent flats, not heartbeat frequency.

### c19 Clawback (Возвратомер) - flat-fee marketplace overcharge and penalty auditor — avg 4.3

- Direction: Solo-business cash flow
- Idea: Connects to a seller's official marketplace API, finds overcharges, lost goods and disputable penalties daily, and drafts each claim before its deadline.
- How: The seller pastes a read-only API token.
